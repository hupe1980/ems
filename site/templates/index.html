Home energy management that obeys the German grid — and proves it.

One number, one sentence, and why

The box serves its own page on the household's network: what the house draws, the reason for every device, the cheap hours ahead, and a receipt for the day against the same house without hems. It notifies a phone when a reduction begins, stays awake on a wall, and prints the installer's handover sheet. Every image here is the real page rendering a simulated day.

Six things you can check, not just believe

Each claim names the mechanism that makes it true and the page that shows the test.

  • 🛡️

    A grid limit nothing can widen

    The § 14a limit lives in a guard plane that runs last and only intersects. A property test over a thousand random households, plans and user overrides holds it.

  • 💬

    Every setpoint says why

    A setpoint cannot be constructed without a reason — the grid, a price, a forecast or the household's own override — so every action on a device can be explained after the fact.

  • ⚖️

    Savings against the same house

    The baseline owns the same battery, run the way it ships, under the same weather and grid rules. Battery wear, comfort and undelivered charge are costs, not footnotes.

  • 📜

    Rules with citations

    § 14a, § 9 EEG, Modul 3, MiSpeL and § 42c as code, each number tagged with its clause — [BK6-22-300 A1 4.5.2] — and the build fails on a citation it cannot resolve.

  • 🏠

    Runs the house without the cloud

    Guard, arbiter and planner run on the box and take time as an input, so a whole winter day with a reduction is a test that runs in milliseconds.

  • 🔐

    The household's data stays the household's

    A local API for the box's own history, a Data Act export on request, and an aggregator credential that can drive devices but cannot read when somebody was home.

Since 2024 a German house is a small power station

Six rules now act on one connection point. hems implements each of them; the background page explains them in ten minutes.

  • § 14a EnWGThe network operator may turn controllable devices down — never below a minimum.
  • § 9 EEGNew roofs feed in 60 % until a smart meter arrives.
  • § 41a EnWGEvery supplier offers a price that moves every quarter hour.
  • Modul 1–3What the household gets back for being controllable.
  • MiSpeLA battery may mix roof and grid energy, if it can tell them apart.
  • § 42c EnWGNeighbours may share a roof's electricity over the grid.

Three planes, one order of authority

A house is decided at three speeds. Each layer may only narrow what the layer above allowed, so “the grid limit was respected” is a property of the structure, not a code path somebody remembered to write.

  • Measured where the rule measures

    § 14a limits what the controllable devices draw from the grid, so a discharging battery lends them headroom. Applying it per device is the obvious implementation, and it costs money.

  • Silence is not zero

    A controllable device the box cannot hear is assumed to draw its nameplate; a silent inverter, to produce nothing. The verdict names every asset it had to assume for.

  • Shared limits are shared

    The main fuse, sub-boards and the 4,6 kVA single-phase limit go through one weighted max-min allocator, so no device is bounded by a limit it shares as if it had it alone.

One day, end to end

A January household — a roof, a 10 kWh battery, a car that must be full by seven, a heat pump, a hot-water tank, a dishwasher — and a network operator's reduction from 17:00 to 18:30. Every line is printed by hemsd simulate --day winter, which CI runs.

hemsd simulate --day winter 
  electricity bill                     20.64 €
  battery life spent                    0.63 €
  comfort given up                      0.22 €
  borrowed from the stores              0.09 €
  cost of the day                      21.58 €
  without optimisation                 22.69 €
  saved                                 1.12 €
  …of it on the bill                    0.66 €

  § 14a limit in force                  90 min
  …against a minimum of                10.5 kW
  …covered by the store                1.4 kWh
  slowest reaction              0 s, commanded
  § 14a limit respected                    yes
  § 9 EEG ceiling respected                yes

  production forecast, CRPS  173 W (83 % of 30 lit)
  load forecast, CRPS      18 W (85 % covered)

  • saved, and …of it on the bill

    The saving charges battery wear, comfort and undelivered service. The bill difference is the flattering number — and here it is the smaller one, because the factory controller cycles the pack harder.

  • without optimisation

    The same service with the same equipment: the same battery on the self-consumption rule hybrid inverters ship with, a wallbox that starts on plug-in, ordinary thermostats, the same weather and grid rules.

  • …covered by the store

    Energy the battery lent the controllable devices during the reduction, which never crossed the connection point and so never counted against the limit.

  • The forecast lines

    The planner only knows what six weeks of the box's own metering could have taught it, and the day is played against weather it never saw. Each day scores its own forecasts beside the money.

Questions to ask any energy manager

The ones that decide whether a household can trust it. Here are hems's answers, each one something you can verify in the source.

Askhems answers
Can a plan, an app or an aggregator push a device past the § 14a limit?No. The guard clamps every instruction and tells an external manager when it did.
What is the saving measured against?The same house, hardware, weather and rules, with wear and comfort charged as costs.
Which clause does this number come from?Every regulatory number carries its citation; the build resolves all {{ config.extra.stat_rules }}.
What happens when the internet is down?The box keeps planning on cached prices and weather and enforces the limit locally.
Why did my battery just discharge?Every setpoint carries its reason, and the box reports it on its local API.
Who can read my consumption history?The household. An aggregator's credential can drive devices, not read the history.
Can I use another energy manager?Yes, over S2 (EN 50491-12-2), with a credential you can withdraw.
Can I read the code that decides?Yes. MIT OR Apache-2.0, and the domain crates are usable on their own.

One box, then ten thousand

Six services stand around the box, and none of them can make the house unsafe or unlawful: the § 14a limit arrives on a wire from the Steuerbox, and prices and weather only ever make a plan better.

Or use one crate on its own

The domain crates do no I/O and take time as a parameter. hems-grid is the German grid rules, hems-tariff what a kilowatt-hour costs, hems-flex a household's flexibility in S2.

use hems_grid::para14a::{ControlMode, SteuVe, minimum_power};
use hems_core::prelude::{AssetId, Fallgruppe, Power};

let devices = [
    SteuVe {
        assets: vec![AssetId::new("wallbox")?],
        fallgruppe: Fallgruppe::Ladepunkt,
        power: Power::from_kw(11.0),
    },
    SteuVe {
        assets: vec![AssetId::new("battery")?],
        fallgruppe: Fallgruppe::Stromspeicher,
        power: Power::from_kw(5.0),
    },
];

// 4,2 kW + (2 − 1) × 0,8 × 4,2 kW = 7,56 kW — the floor the
// network operator may not go below, [BK6-22-300 A1 4.5.2].
let floor = minimum_power(&devices, ControlMode::Ems);
assert!((floor.kw() - 7.56).abs() < 1e-9);
